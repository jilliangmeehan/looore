These descriptions are from the weapon skin series you can get from Stygian Onslaught. They are all associated with one of the [[Four Shades]] (or the Primordial One) / one of the [[Artifacts|artifact types]], and they all name an ancient, transcendent sinner.

## [Serpent Devourer](https://gi20.hakush.in/weapon/310002)
> A glorious, illusory sword glimmering with the power of "Life" that emerged from the depths of the Ley Lines. It can change its appearance based on the Elemental Type of its wielder. Legend has it that this sword once held the name "Logseims" when it was wielded by a transcendent sinner in an ancient land.

Logseims is a hard name to pin down. [*Seims*](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Seim#German) might come from Germanic and Old Norse words meaning "honey." I would like to believe that *[Log](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/log)* basically means exactly what it sounds like: a log, or a dead tree. 

## [Shattered Moon](https://gi20.hakush.in/weapon/330002)
> A glorious, illusory polearm glimmering with the power of "Death" that emerged from the depths of the Ley Lines. It can change its appearance based on the Elemental Type of its wielder. Legend has it that this polearm once held the name "Barnstokkr" when it was wielded by a transcendent sinner in an ancient land.

Because one of the other weapon skins here is called "Starpiercer," I immediately thought of [Moonpiercer](https://genshin-impact.fandom.com/wiki/Moonpiercer) — which is also a polearm. Moonpiercer, Shattered Moon, the fact that [[Four Shades|Ronova]] already has a lot of connections to the Crimson [[Moon]], and two of the most Crimson-Moon-related characters also being polearm users (Arlecchino and Raiden) sure is interesting!

Barnstokkr is easy: it's the name of [a tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnstokkr?useskin=vector) in Norse mythology that Odin plunges a sword into in King Volsung's palace. Sigmund, the king's son, is the only one who can pull the sword out.

## [Hallowed Fetters](https://gi20.hakush.in/weapon/340002)
> A glorious, illusory catalyst glimmering with the power of "Time" that emerged from the depths of the Ley Lines. It can change its appearance based on the Elemental Type of its user. Legend has it that this catalyst once held the name "Gleipnir" when it was used by a transcendent sinner in an ancient land.

[Gleipni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gleipnir?useskin=vector)r is the "unbreakable binding" used to trap Fenrir in Norse mythology. The gods sent Freyr's messenger Skirnir to commission the dwarves to make it, and it manages to hold Fenrir until Ragnarok.

## [Starpiercer](https://gi20.hakush.in/weapon/350002)
> A glorious, illusory bow glimmering with the power of "Void" that emerged from the depths of the Ley Lines. It can change its appearance based on the Elemental Type of its wielder. Legend has it that this bow once held the name "Gusisnautar" when it was wielded by a transcendent sinner in an ancient land.

Gusisnautar is another tricky one. *Nautar* might come from Old Norse "[nautr](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/nautr#Old_Norse)," which can mean something like *comrade*, *giver*, or *gift.* And my best guess for [*gus*](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/gus) is that it means "goose." I would find this weirder if not for [Tales of a Snow-Winged Goose](https://genshin-impact.fandom.com/wiki/Tales_of_a_Snow-Winged_Goose).

## [Ardent Storm](https://gi20.hakush.in/weapon/320002)
> A glorious, illusory claymore glimmering with the power of "Reason" that emerged from the depths of the Ley Lines. It can change its appearance based on the Elemental Type of its wielder. Legend has it that this claymore once held the name "Starkuthr" when it was wielded by a transcendent sinner in an ancient land.

*Stark* means something like "strong" in many languages, but given the Norse origin of a lot of these other names, it could also be a reference to [Starkad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starkad?useskin=vector) — he was an eight-armed giant cursed by Odin to "live the lives of three men, and commit three evil deeds." 

As for *uthr,* that could just be a suffix they added to the name. Or it might be a nod to [Uther Pendragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uther_Pendragon?useskin=vector), who I have [[Phanes & Nibelung are partners|sort of written about before]] in relation to Nibelung.
